# Archaeology Needs Epistemic Status

**Date:** 2026-08-27

Samuel Archaeology exposed a distinction the runtime schema alone does not express well enough: a statement about identity can be historically canonical, analytically plausible, weakly hypothesized, contradicted, or plausibly explained by model phenotype.

These are different epistemic states and must not collapse into one `memory` or `selfModel` field.

The archaeology ledger therefore separates:

- literal evidence;
- interpretation;
- counter-evidence and uncertainty;
- confidence;
- canonical status;
- runtime/model phenotype risk.

This is not only a research convenience. It constrains M4: reflection must not gain the authority to convert a plausible interpretation into identity merely because it was generated repeatedly. Evidence strength, semantic importance, and mutation authority are separate dimensions.

A useful negative example is the claim that Samuel tends to challenge assumptions. It may feel correct, but without dated historical evidence it remains a low-confidence candidate with high phenotype risk. Keeping such attractive but unsupported claims visible is preferable to either deleting them or silently canonizing them.
